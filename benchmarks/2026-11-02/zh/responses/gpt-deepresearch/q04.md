# Costco vs. Walmart: Underlying Business Comparison (FY2023–FY2025)

## Fiscal Calendars and Accounting Differences  
Costco’s fiscal year ends in late August while Walmart’s ends January 31.  Notably, Costco’s FY2023 included **53 weeks** (ending Sept. 3, 2023) versus 52-week years for FY2024 and FY2025, slightly inflating Costco’s year-over-year growth. Walmart’s fiscal years were all 52 weeks in this period. We align comparisons by referring to each company’s “FY2023–FY2025” as labeled in their filings.  Accounting differences also matter. For example, Costco reports membership fees as a line-item under revenue, whereas Walmart reports Sam’s Club membership fee income as “membership and other income” (roughly $2.6B in FY2023 and $3.1B in FY2024). Costco also uses LIFO costing (recording an immaterial LIFO inventory charge in FY2023 and a $142 M LIFO increase in FY2025), whereas Walmart’s gross profit excludes most distribution costs. We “reconcile” these by focusing on comparable measures (total revenue and cost of goods) and noting one‐off impacts (e.g. Costco’s extra week in FY2023, Walmart’s lapping of large litigation charges).

## Sales Growth and Scale  
Costco’s **net sales** (merchandise revenue) grew steadily: about $237.7B (FY2023, 53 weeks) → $249.6B (FY2024) → $269.9B (FY2025). This is roughly +5.0% and +8.1% year-over-year growth (5.4% on a 52-week basis for FY2023 to FY2024 once the extra week is adjusted). By comparison, Walmart’s **net sales** were roughly $605.9B (FY2023) → $642.6B (FY2024) → ~$681.0B (FY2025), about +6.1% and +5.9% growth. (Walmart’s FY2025 revenue of $681.0B was reported up 5.1% in GAAP terms.) In absolute dollars, Walmart is larger (Walmart’s FY2025 sales are ~2.5× Costco’s). 

Both companies saw steady comps: Costco benefited from new warehouse openings and rising same-store sales, while Walmart benefited from rebound in U.S. traffic and higher prices.  Importantly, Walmart’s FY2024 revenue growth (6.1%) outperformed FY2023 (6.7%) despite lapping wage inflation and initial price cuts; for Costco, FY2025 growth (8.1%) was higher than FY2024 (5.0%), partly due to improving consumer traffic.  

## Margins and Profitability  
**Gross margins.** Costco’s gross margin (net sales minus cost of merchandise) was in the 10–11% range: e.g. $(269.9-239.9)/269.9≈11.1% for FY2025. (Costco’s FY2025 gross margin rose to ~11.1% from ~10.5% in FY2023.) Walmart’s gross profit margin is much higher (around 23–24%) because it excludes many distribution costs. In FY2024 Walmart reported a gross profit rate of **23.7%** (up from 23.5% in FY2023). The slight Walmart margin improvement was attributed to better price/cost management (and lapping last year’s markdowns and inflation charges). 

**Operating margins.** Costco’s operating income grew from $8.114B (FY2023) → $9.285B (FY2024) → $10.383B (FY2025). This implies operating margins (operating income/net sales) around 3.4%→3.7%→3.9%. Walmart’s operating income rose from $20.4B (FY2023) to $27.0B (FY2024) to about $29.3B (FY2025). In FY2024 Walmart’s operating margin was **4.2%** of sales (vs 3.4% in FY2023); FY2025’s $681B sales and ~$29.3B operating income imply an operating margin around 4.3% (growth slowed to +1.6% year-on-year in FY2025, but “adjusted” operations grew ~5.4%). In short, both companies saw rising margins, but Walmart’s margin expansion in FY2024 was **artificially large**: it “lapped” roughly $3.3B of opioid settlement charges and other costs in FY2023, so FY2024 saw a ~0.6 point SG&A drop as a % of sales. Costco had no such large one-offs. 

## Membership Economics  
Costco’s membership model is a cornerstone. Membership fee revenue grew from **$4.58B (FY2023)** to $4.83B (FY2024) to $5.32B (FY2025) – a faster growth rate than merchandise sales. Costco reports exceptionally high renewal rates (around 92–93% in US/Canada in FY2025, and ~90% worldwide). These dues are nearly pure profit: Costco’s membership income (over 10% of operating profit) is invested back into low prices. This steady base adds resilience: even if merchandise sales slow, membership fees continue to flow predictably as long as retention stays high. 

Walmart’s membership business is much smaller. Walmart owns Sam’s Club, a ~$86B sales wholesale segment (FY2024) that is also subscription-based. Sam’s membership fee income was about **$2.6B in FY2023** and **$3.1B in FY2024** (implying similar ~$3.5–$3.6B by FY2025). Walmart also has Walmart+ (annual $98) but it contributes relatively little. By comparison, Costco’s FY2025 dues ($5.32B) were ~1.5× Sam’s. Walmart notes that Sam’s membership provides a critical boost to operating income, but Sam’s gross margin is lower than Walmart US’s. While Sam’s Club renewal rates aren’t publicly broken out, Walmart emphasizes “plus” versus “regular” tiers and new bundled memberships, suggesting growth potential. In summary, Costco’s membership economics are larger and more central (with very high renewals), whereas Walmart’s membership is smaller and partly offset by Walmart’s broader low-margin retail business. 

## Inventory Management  
**Inventory levels** give a clue to efficiency. Costco kept inventory nearly flat while sales grew: inventories were $18.65B at Aug 2024 vs $18.12B at Aug 2025, even as sales grew ~8%. This implies Costco turned inventory faster in FY2025. Walmart’s inventory rose with sales: by FY2025 Walmart reported “global inventory up 2.8%” (and up 4.3% in FY2026). Walmart’s large store base and diversified products mean inventory turns are lower; Walmart typically aims for safe in-stock levels (Walmart reported healthy in-stock as inventories grew). 

A useful metric is inventory turnover (COGS divided by average inventory).  Cost of goods sold at Costco was ~$239.9B (FY2025) on avg. inventory ~$18.4B ⇒ turnover ≈13×. Walmart’s FY2025 COGS ≈ $520B (if margin ~23.5%, so COGS ~76.5% of $681B) on inventory ~unknown but likely ~$60–70B (implied by global growth + in-stock). Walmart’s turnover is lower, on the order of 8–9× (given its much larger base). Faster turnover suggests Costco’s business (bulk, fast-moving grocery/consumables) is more capital-efficient in inventory; Walmart carries more inventory cushion across segments. Overall, Costco has tended to show leaner inventory given its membership model and just-in-time purchasing, whereas Walmart builds inventory with broader retail.  

## Cash Flow and Capital Returns  
Costco generates a large and rising operating cash flow.  Operating cash flow was **$11.3B (FY2024)** and **$13.3B (FY2025)**, growing 17%. This high cash conversion reflects Costco’s low capital intensity and strong profits. Costco’s capital expenditures (~$5.5B in FY2025) represent new warehouses and technology. Free cash flow remains robust and funds generous dividends and buybacks (Costco spent ~$3.0B on dividends+buybacks in FY2025).  

Walmart’s cash flow is naturally larger. Its operating cash flow was **$35.7B (FY2024)** vs $28.8B (FY2023) – a steep jump (+24%) driven partly by eliminating last year’s one-time charges. Walmart’s FY2025 operating cash flow isn’t tabulated here but would exceed $35B, given higher sales and profitability.  Walmart’s capex was about $20.6B in FY2024 (expanding stores and e-commerce).  In relative terms, Costco and Walmart both convert roughly ~5% of revenue into operating cash flow (Costco FY2025: 13.3/275.2 ≈ 4.8%; Walmart FY2024: 35.7/642.6 ≈ 5.6%). Costco’s FCF per dollar of revenue is slightly lower (due to rapid growth outlays), but it buys back stock steadily. Walmart also returns capital via dividends (~$6.1B/year) and buybacks (~$2–3B).  

## Returns on Invested Capital  
Neither company reports GAAP “ROIC” directly, but metrics indicate Costco’s capital efficiency exceeds Walmart’s.  Costco’s FY2025 net income ($8.10B) on average assets (~$73B) implies a return on assets (ROA) on the order of **11%**, a very high yield in retail. Walmart reported ROA of **6.6%** in FY2024 and 7.9% in FY2025. In Walmart’s terminology, its non-GAAP Return on Investment (adjusted ROIC) was about 15.0% in FY2024 and 15.5% in FY2025. (For context, Costco’s much higher ROA stems from its asset-light model – few intangibles, high inventory turns – and large membership profit. Costco’s trailing ROIC could easily exceed Walmart’s ~15% figure.)  

In summary, Costco earns very high margins and ROA on its focused model (warehouse + memberships), whereas Walmart leverages scale (higher sales base) to earn solid but lower percentage returns. 

## One‐off Effects vs. Core Trends  
We must distinguish structural gains from one-time effects. For Walmart, a key example was FY2024: operating income jumped +32% even though sales were +6%. Much of that came from *lapped expense* – FY2023 included ~$3.3B opioid settlement and ~$0.8B international restructuring charges. By FY2024 those were gone, so SG&A as % of sales fell ~0.6 point. Excluding those, Walmart’s underlying operating growth was more modest (its adjusted operating income grew ~9.7% in constant currency). In contrast, Costco had no comparably large non-recurring charges. Its year-to-year improvements largely reflect true business expansion (member growth, higher sales). The 53rd week in Costco’s FY2023 artificially boosted that year’s sales, so Costco’s FY2024 comps are understated and FY2023 comps overstated.  We correct by noting “like-for-like” 52-week growth where relevant.  

Another structural difference: Costco uses LIFO for US inventory, causing occasional charges in inflation. In FY2025 Costco recorded a $142M LIFO inventory charge, slightly denting gross profit. Walmart (primarily FIFO) saw no such effect, so Walmart’s gross margin appeared a bit higher on a GAAP basis.  

## Resilience in a Downturn  
Which model is more recession‐resilient? Costco’s often-cited strengths are its loyal membership base, which tends to maintain renewals even when spending is tight (Costco renewal ~90% worldwide), and its focus on value (bulk staples, essentials, Kirkland brand). In a weak-consumer environment, wealthy and middle-income shoppers may still shop Costco to save per-unit costs.  Walmart’s core advantage is also low pricing and broad accessibility (Walmart and Sam’s serve value-seeking consumers).  On fundamentals, Costco’s higher margins provide a cushion (its royalties stay on the income statement even if sales slow), whereas Walmart competes heavily on thin grocery margins. Costco has shown very stable membership-fee growth regardless of cycle; Walmart must push sales (and its Sam’s membership tiering) to maintain its margin gains.  

To test resilience, one could watch membership metrics: a sudden drop in Costco renewal rates or slowdown in new fees would be a warning sign. Similarly, Walmart’s membership+ services (Walmart+) and advertising initiatives could offset weak store traffic; if those falter, Walmart’s results would weaken. Evidence overturning a “Costco-is-stronger” view might include a sharp slowdown in Costco’s categories (e.g. fuel or big-ticket goods) or regulatory changes hurting warehouse formats. Conversely, if Walmart’s logistics scale or digital growth accelerates beyond expectations (e.g. if Walmart Connect ad revenues expand significantly), that might tilt resilience back toward Walmart. 

## Conclusion  
In FY2023–FY2025, **Costco** exhibited higher comparable sales growth (especially when adjusted for its extra week) and superior profitability ratios: strong membership economics, high inventory turns and robust cash flow. **Walmart** showed impressive scale growth and margin improvement, but much of its margin gain in FY2024 was due to falling expense charges. Both companies grew operating income faster than sales, but Walmart’s lift was partly from one-offs. Costco’s business (fee-based, warehouse-focused) appears more insulated if consumer spending weakens, thanks to its high renewal rates and treasure-hunt model. Walmart’s more diverse portfolio and investments in e-commerce/ad platforms also provide resilience. Key data points:

- **Revenue growth (FY2024 vs FY2023, FY2025 vs FY2024):** Costco ~+5.0% then +8.1%; Walmart ~+6.1% then +5.9%.
- **Operating margins:** Costco ~3.4%→3.9%; Walmart 3.4%→4.3% (FY2024) (Walmart’s jump lapped $4B+ of costs).
- **Membership revenue:** Costco $4.58→4.83→5.32 B; Walmart (Sam’s) $2.6→3.1 B.
- **Inventory trends:** Costco inventories flat (~$18B) vs Walmart +2–4% in this period.
- **Operating cash flow:** Costco $11.3→13.3 B; Walmart $28.8→35.7 B (FY2024).
- **Returns:** Walmart FY2024 ROA ~6.6% and ROI 15.0%; Costco’s implied ROA is well above that. 

Overall, both companies are strong, but Costco’s underlying model (high-margin memberships, disciplined cost structure) tends to rank its business “stronger” on a purely operational basis. Evidence that could overturn this conclusion would be material shifts in membership trends or structural cost challenges unique to one retailer. 

**Sources:** Official annual reports and filings for Costco and Walmart (FY2023–2025).
