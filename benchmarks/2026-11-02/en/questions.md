## 1. Bristol Home Battery Decision

I own a house in Bristol and am considering adding a home battery to my existing 4 kWp solar system. My household uses about 4,500 kWh of electricity a year, mostly in the evening, and we do not have an EV or a heat pump. The solar system generates roughly 3,600 kWh a year, of which we currently use 35% directly. For an initial calculation, assume imported electricity costs 27p/kWh and exported electricity earns 15p/kWh. Using products, installation costs and UK rules available as of 29 October 2026, would a battery of around 5 kWh or 10 kWh make financial sense, or would I be better off keeping the current setup? Please account for usable capacity, conversion losses, degradation, warranties and the export income I would give up, and explain whether an off-peak tariff could materially change the decision. I would like to understand which conclusions follow from these numbers and which depend on my actual half-hourly consumption and solar generation.

## 3. Search Architecture for a B2B Knowledge Product

I lead a six-person engineering team building search for a B2B knowledge product. We already use PostgreSQL and expect about five million text chunks, each with a 1,536-dimensional embedding, spread across 2,000 customer organizations. Every search must respect organization and document permissions. We need keyword search, semantic search and frequent document updates, with around 50 searches per second at peak and a target end-to-end p95 latency below 300 ms. As of 29 October 2026, should we start with PostgreSQL and pgvector, use OpenSearch, or run Qdrant alongside PostgreSQL? Please assess hybrid ranking, filtered vector search, permission enforcement, index and memory requirements, update consistency, backup and recovery, and the operational burden for a team of our size. Public benchmarks often seem to disagree; which are relevant to this workload, what would we need to measure ourselves, and what signs would justify moving away from the initial choice?
