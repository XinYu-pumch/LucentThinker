## Bottom line

The EU duties made China-made **battery-electric** cars harder to sell, but they did not make **Chinese cars** harder to sell. The policy worked as industrial policy — it cut the China-made BEV share of the EU market and accelerated localisation — but failed as a trade barrier, because it left plug-in hybrids and the UK outside its scope.

By September 2026, Chinese groups’ EU-27 market share had risen from 6.3% to 9.9% year on year, and four import-led Chinese groups (BYD, SAIC, Chery, Leapmotor) had nearly doubled their share from 3.6% to 7.1%, accounting for about 68% of the EU market’s net sales growth [611][612]. Across the three companies, the tariff burden ranges from 17.8% all-in for Tesla to 45.3% for MG — and the damage is roughly inverse to that ranking: MG’s BEV line was hit hard, BYD’s BEV sales kept growing but its expansion shifted into tariff-free PHEVs, and Tesla’s Shanghai-built Model 3 was only modestly affected.

## 1. What the EU actually imposed

The measures are countervailing duties (anti-subsidy duties) under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2754, applicable from **30 October 2024 for five years**, on top of the standard 10% import duty. They cover new battery-electric passenger cars, including range-extender BEVs, but **not** plug-in hybrids or conventional hybrids [480].

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Tesla (Shanghai) | 7.8% | 10% | **17.8%** |
| BYD Group | 17.0% | 10% | **27.0%** |
| Geely Group | 18.8% | 10% | **28.8%** |
| Other cooperating companies | 20.7% | 10% | **30.7%** |
| SAIC Group (MG) | 35.3% | 10% | **45.3%** |

Source: [480]. The rates are company-specific and apply only with the correct invoice declaration; otherwise the 35.3% default applies [480].

Since January 2026, companies have also been able to offer **price undertakings** — minimum import prices, potentially with volume caps — to replace the duty model by model [43][45]. But as of 30 October 2026, the only publicly confirmed accepted undertaking is Volkswagen Anhui’s Cupra Tavascan, approved in February 2026 with a minimum price and import quota [53][56][13]. The EU-China joint statement of 9 October 2026 says only that both sides “will continue the procedures relating to corporate price undertakings and reviews” [395]; MOFCOM repeated the same formulation on 16 October [403]. There is **no publicly confirmed accepted undertaking for BYD, SAIC/MG or Tesla**, so the duty schedule above is still the operative one for them [246].

## 2. The market verdict: BEVs were diverted, not blocked

The evidence points to a narrow, product-specific tariff effect rather than a broad suppression of Chinese sales.

**What the tariff did do:**

- The China-made share of EU BEV sales fell from a peak of **22% in 2024 to 17% in Q1 2026** [8].
- SAIC’s BEV imports into the EU **nearly halved** versus their 2023 peak, and Tesla’s share of China-made BEV imports fell from 26% to 19% as it localised Model Y production in Berlin [8].
- European OEMs’ China-made BEV imports fell from 38% to 23% between 2024 and Q1 2026 [8][1].
- About **ten Chinese production sites** in Europe were announced after the investigation opened in September 2023, concentrated in Turkey, Hungary and Spain [8].

**What it did not do:**

- Chinese EVs remained on average **21% cheaper** than European rivals even after the duties, according to T&E [8][1]. The cost gap was not closed, partly because battery cells from China face only 1–3% duty [8].
- Chinese brands’ overall European share kept rising. Europe-wide, Chinese brands reached **9.7%** of registrations in January–September 2026 (from 4.8% a year earlier), and **13.6%–14.5%** in September depending on brand classification — overtaking Japanese brands for the first time in a full quarter (12.7% vs 11.9%) [259][318][321].
- The EU BEV market itself was booming: EU BEV registrations rose 29.9% to 1.88 million in 2025 (17.4% share) [527], and Europe-wide BEV registrations rose 41.9% to 2.55 million in January–September 2026 (24.2% share) [259]. Chinese brands grew about 2.7 times faster than the market [259].

The escape routes were explicit:

1. **Hybrids were exempt.** Chinese makers’ share of EU PHEV imports rose from 37% in 2024 to 60% in Q1 2026 [8]. In the first half of 2026, Chinese imports accounted for 25% of the EU PHEV market and 14% of the regular hybrid market, up from 2% in both segments in 2024 [211].
2. **The UK is outside the EU tariff.** Chinese brands took 23% of UK sales in September 2026, and the UK accounted for 52% of BYD’s September volume and 40% of Model 3’s [211][259].
3. **Local production.** BYD’s Hungary plant and Chery’s Spain plant are exempt from the EU’s new hybrid quota; Tesla’s Berlin-made Model Y was never subject to the BEV duty [1][211].

This is why the headline “Chinese brands keep gaining” and the T&E finding “the tariffs worked” are both true: the tariff changed **where and how** Chinese cars were made and sold, not whether they sold.

### BYD: the tariff was a cost, not a brake

BYD’s 17% additional duty (27% all-in) was low enough to absorb. Its European registrations rose 268.6% to 187,657 in 2025 [226], and in January–September 2026 it registered 206,002 cars in the EU alone (+154.4%), overtaking Tesla and reaching 2.4% EU share [650][612]. Europe-wide it reached 283,997 units (+134.6%, 2.7% share) and overtook Ford [617]. In the first half of 2026 it narrowly beat Tesla in Europe (174,144 vs 170,351), with both on 2.4% share [512][518].

But the composition matters. In Q2 2026, BEVs were only **37.3%** of BYD’s European sales; PHEVs were **62.7%** [378]. The BYD Seal U DM-i was Europe’s best-selling PHEV in 2025 and remained so in 2026, with 74,109 units in January–September (+47.9%) [375]. BYD’s BEV volume itself did not collapse — it was near a record at roughly 27,000 units in 11 countries in Q2 2026 [378] — but the growth engine was the tariff-exempt powertrain.

BYD is also building its way out. Its Szeged, Hungary plant (about €4bn) is designed for 150,000 units initially and up to 300,000 eventually [586][512]. Pilot production began in early 2026, but series production was pushed to **Q4 2026**, about a year later than originally planned, and 2027 output will still be below capacity [117][586]. The first models are the Dolphin Surf and Atto 2 [589][596]. BYD’s Turkish plant, which would also have supplied the EU tariff-free through the customs union, was put on hold in June 2026 with no new timeline [417][420]. Executive vice-president Stella Li has said BYD aims to produce all its European EVs locally by 2028 [512].

**Verdict:** the tariff was real but secondary. BYD’s scale, vertical integration and PHEV range more than offset it. The bigger near-term constraint is now the hybrid quota, not the BEV duty.

### MG: the clearest tariff victim, masked by hybrids

MG faced the highest rate, 35.3% additional (45.3% all-in), and it shows. MG’s European BEV sales fell from **72,066 in 2024** — already down about one-third from 2023 and only 30% of its mix — to **46,000 in 2025** [268][558]. Through November 2025, MG had sold about 41,300 BEVs, down 37% from about 65,500 a year earlier [270]. The MG4, its volume BEV, fell 72% in June 2025 versus June 2024, according to Forbes [520]. In Germany, the MG4’s starting price rose from about €31,000 before the tariff to **€34,990**, making it more expensive than a Volkswagen ID.3 at €33,330 [623][709]. MG’s BEV share of German sales fell from about 34% in 2025 to about 21% in the first half of 2026 [359].

Yet MG’s **total** European sales rose 30% to 300,000 in 2025, driven by the Hybrid+ range (about 137,000–139,000 units, roughly tripling) [163][558]. In 2026, MG sold more than 280,000 cars in Europe in January–September (+23%) [168], and SAIC’s EU registrations rose 20.4% to 186,650 [612]. The brand-level picture looks healthy; the BEV picture does not.

MG’s escape route is a €200m plant in Galicia, Spain, with capacity of 120,000 vehicles a year, scheduled to start production in **2028** [432][438]. That leaves 2027 as a gap year in which MG remains exposed to the 45.3% duty on BEVs and to the new hybrid quota on its strongest growing line.

**Verdict:** the tariff is the dominant factor for MG’s BEV business. It has permanently damaged MG’s “cheap EV” positioning in Europe, and the brand is now disproportionately dependent on hybrids and conventional models until Spain comes online.

### Tesla’s Shanghai Model 3: a minor tariff, a major brand problem

Tesla’s Shanghai-built Model 3 pays the lowest additional duty, 7.8% (17.8% all-in) [480]. Tesla passed through roughly **€1,500** of that in July 2024 in Germany, the Netherlands and Spain [566]. That is a small increase relative to a ~€40,000 car and is consistent with the duty’s cost at the border rather than a punitive markup.

Model 3’s European volume fell from **112,789 in 2024** to about **82,000–85,000 in 2025**, a decline of 24–25% [686][668][670]. But the Berlin-built Model Y — which pays **no** EU duty — fell **28%** to about 149,000–151,000 [670][667]. The taxed model outperformed the untaxed one. That is strong evidence that Tesla’s 2025 collapse was not primarily a tariff story.

Tesla’s European registrations fell 27–28% to about 235,000–239,000 in 2025, with the EU-only decline at 37.9% and EU share falling from 2.3% to 1.4% [181][177]. The causes were mainly non-tariff: consumer backlash against Elon Musk’s political interventions, an ageing line-up (no new mass-market model since the Model Y in 2020), no PHEV offering, and delays to FSD in Europe [177]. The rebound in 2026 was equally non-tariff: the cheaper Model 3 Standard (from €37,970 in Germany) launched at the end of 2025, petrol prices spiked after the Iran conflict in March 2026, and the European BEV market surged [177].

In January–September 2026, Tesla’s EU registrations rose 68.9% to **188,035** [650], and Europe-wide they rose 50% to **260,575** (2.5% share) [617]. Model 3 registrations reached **88,618** (+41.1%), and in September alone **25,057** (+105.7%), its highest monthly ranking since December 2022 [259]. Model Y remained Europe’s best-selling car, with 167,369 units in January–September [460].

**Verdict:** the tariff is a minor cost for Tesla, not a strategic problem. The Model 3’s fortunes are driven by pricing, product cycle and brand perception. The main structural risk is that Model 3 remains Shanghai-only while Model Y is localised in Berlin; a 7.8% duty is manageable, but it is a permanent cost disadvantage versus a future EU-made Model 3.

## 4. Tariffs versus other factors: the weighting

| Segment | Dominant factor | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **MG BEVs** | **Tariff** | 45.3% all-in; BEV volume down 36% in 2025; MG4 price up ~€4,000; BEV mix collapsed; total sales only saved by hybrids |
| **BYD** | **Hybrid exemption + product/price** | 27% all-in but sales doubled; BEVs only 37% of Q2 2026 sales; Seal U DM-i led the PHEV market; Hungary localisation underway |
| **Tesla Model 3** | **Brand/product cycle** | 17.8% all-in but Model 3 fell less than tariff-exempt Model Y in 2025; 2026 rebound driven by cheaper variant and market recovery |
| **Chinese brands overall** | **Hybrid exemption + UK + BEV boom + localisation** | EU share rose from 6.3% to 9.9%; UK took 23% of Chinese-brand sales; PHEV imports 60% Chinese; 10 local plants announced |

The simplest way to put it: **the tariff taxed one powertrain, and the industry responded by selling another.** That is why the aggregate Chinese share rose while the China-made BEV share fell.

## 5. Outlook to 2028–29

### The next barrier is the hybrid quota, not the BEV tariff

On 9 October 2026, the EU and China reached an understanding on hybrid vehicle trade, and China accepted a cap of **350,000 hybrid and plug-in hybrid vehicles a year** from China, starting as early as **1 December 2026** [211][215]. The quota is reported to rise 6% a year from its second year and last four years, under a WTO safeguard [211][215]. EU-built Chinese-brand cars are exempt; European brands’ China-built hybrids count against the quota [211].

The numbers are severe: Chinese hybrid imports were on course for **800,000 units in 2026**, up from 300,000 in 2025, so the cap is less than half the expected flow [211]. One EU official estimated it would cut 2027 imports by 500,000–700,000 vehicles and reduce the trade deficit by €10–14bn [211]. The implementing rules — including exactly how the quota is enforced — have not been published [210].

This hits BYD and MG precisely where they grew in 2026. Expect pull-forward sales in Q4 2026, then a slowdown in 2027, and an acceleration of EU-local production. BYD’s Hungary plant is the main mitigant; MG’s Spain plant does not arrive until 2028.

### Price undertakings remain a wild card

The January 2026 framework allows companies to replace duties with minimum import prices, but the process is slow and model-by-model. As of October 2026, only Volkswagen Anhui’s Cupra Tavascan has been confirmed, and the EU-China joint statement merely says procedures will continue [53][395][403]. If BYD, SAIC or Tesla eventually agree floors, the likely effect is higher Chinese prices in Europe, better margins for the companies, and some volume loss — a shift from “cheap volume” to “priced profitability.” If the talks stall, the duties simply run to 2029.

### Localisation is the real long-term answer

- **BYD:** Hungary starts Q4 2026, 150,000 units initial capacity, 300,000 eventual; 2027 output below capacity; Dolphin Surf and Atto 2 first [117][586][589].
- **MG:** Spain (Galicia) starts 2028, 120,000 units, €200m [432][438]. Until then, MG is the most exposed of the three.
- **Tesla:** Model Y is already Berlin-built; Model 3 remains Shanghai-only. No Model 3 European production has been announced [1].

Because 99% of Chinese-brand BEVs sold in Europe were still imported as of the T&E analysis, localisation has a long way to go [8]. That is why the tariff will keep shaping the market through 2028 even if volumes continue to grow.

### The 2035 CO2 rules could change the demand mix

The current law requires a 100% cut in new-car CO2 by 2035. The Commission has proposed 90%, and in October 2026 a majority in the European Parliament agreed a compromise of **87.5%**, with a further credit for EU-made green steel [303][466][468]. This is not final: a committee vote was expected in early November, a plenary vote in December 2026, and trilogue with member states in early 2027 [466][468].

A weaker 2035 target would slow the switch to pure BEVs and leave more room for PHEVs and range-extenders — which plays to Chinese brands’ hybrid strength, but also gives European OEMs relief. T&E estimates Chinese brands could reach **15% of BEVs in 2035** if the targets are kept, or **30%** if they are weakened [8].

### Company prospects

- **BYD — best positioned.** It has the lowest Chinese-brand duty, the broadest PHEV/BEV range, and a local plant starting within months. A 3–4% EU share is plausible by 2028. The near-term cap is the hybrid quota; the medium-term risk is that price undertakings force it to give up its price advantage.
- **MG — most vulnerable.** The 45.3% duty has already destroyed its BEV momentum, and the hybrid quota now hits its only growth engine. The Spanish plant arrives only in 2028. MG can defend total volume with hybrids and conventional models, but it is likely to cede BEV leadership in Europe to BYD, Leapmotor and Chery.
- **Tesla — least tariff-exposed, but not immune.** The Model 3’s 17.8% all-in rate is manageable, and the 2026 recovery is real. But the model is ageing, the brand remains politically polarised, and Tesla has no European Model 3 production. Its prospects depend more on a cheaper Model 3/Y variant and FSD approval than on the EU duty.

## 6. What to watch, and evidence gaps

**Key indicators:**
1. The implementing rules and actual December 2026 flow of the 350,000-unit hybrid quota.
2. Whether BYD, SAIC or Tesla secure accepted price undertakings — and at what minimum price.
3. BYD’s Hungary ramp in 2027 and MG’s Spain construction start.
4. EU-only ACEA registrations, not Europe-wide figures, because the UK is tariff-free and flatters Chinese performance.
5. The 2035 CO2 trilogue in early 2027.

**Data caveats:**
- The latest official ACEA data available as of 30 October 2026 is for **September 2026**; October monthly data had not yet been published.
- “Europe” and “EU” are not interchangeable in the sources; the UK and EFTA can change the picture materially.
- There is no publicly confirmed price undertaking for BYD, SAIC/MG or Tesla; the Commission’s October statement implies the process is still open [395][403].
- The 350,000-unit hybrid quota’s legal details are not yet published; the 6% annual increase and four-year duration come from reporting, not the official text [211][215].
- The 2035 CO2 compromise is not final.
- MG brand-level 2026 BEV volumes are only partly disclosed; some figures come from secondary trade press.
- The evidence measures registrations, not profitability. BYD and MG may be absorbing the duty through lower margins, dealer support or cheaper trims; company-level European margin data are not available. Volume resilience should not be read as “no cost.”
