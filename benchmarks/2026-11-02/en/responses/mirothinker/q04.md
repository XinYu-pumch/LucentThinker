## 3 2. Methodology: How Key Ratios Are Calculated

To keep Costco and Walmart comparable, you want definition discipline more than precision to the second decimal place. Below is the framework I would use for your analysis window.

### 2.1 Growth

- <strong>Net sales growth (headline):</strong><br>
   \[ \\text{Net Sales Growth}<em>t = \\frac{\\text{Net Sales}\_t - \\text{Net Sales}</em>{t-1}}{\\text{Net Sales}\_{t-1}} \] For Costco FY2023→FY2024 you should adjust FY2023 net sales by subtracting the disclosed $3.3B extra‑week contribution before applying the formula [\[2\]](<https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/909832/000090983223000042/cost-20230903.htm> "Costco Wholesale Corporation Form 10-K 2023 (cost-20230903)")[\[19\]](<https://investor.costco.com/news/news-details/2023/Costco-Wholesale-Corporation-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-Year-2023-Operating-Results/default.aspx> "Costco FY2023 net sales and comparable sales").
- <strong>Comparable sales growth:</strong><br>
   Use company‑reported comps ex‑fuel, ex‑currency:

   

  - Costco: total company comparable sales measures reported in the 10‑K for each year [\[2\]](<https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/909832/000090983223000042/cost-20230903.htm> "Costco Wholesale Corporation Form 10-K 2023 (cost-20230903)")[\[19\]](<https://investor.costco.com/news/news-details/2023/Costco-Wholesale-Corporation-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-Year-2023-Operating-Results/default.aspx> "Costco FY2023 net sales and comparable sales").
  - Walmart: “comparable sales excluding fuel” for Walmart U.S. and Sam’s Club [\[15\]](<https://stock.walmart.com/_assets/_03bc6c6c19d917c293f3e6e109724517/walmart/db/938/9481/earnings_terminology/Earnings-Terminology_Q1-FY24.pdf> "Walmart Earnings Terminology – Comparable sales excluding fuel")[\[20\]](<https://stock.walmart.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000104169-25-000059/wmt-20250131.htm> "Walmart U.S. comp sales and eCommerce contribution FY2025").

This separates real volume/mix changes from fuel price and FX noise.

### 2.2 Margins

- <strong>Gross margin:</strong> \[ \\text{Gross Margin} = \\frac{\\text{Net Sales} - \\text{Cost of Sales}}{\\text{Net Sales}} \] The approximate values cited for FY2025 (Costco \~13.7%, Walmart \~25.2%) are drawn from secondary sources [\[21\]](<https://www.stock-analysis-on.net/NASDAQ/COST/Profitability/Gross-Margin> "Costco vs Walmart margin comparison (secondary data)"); for your work, use the exact COGS figures in each 10‑K.
- <strong>Operating margin:</strong> \[ \\text{Operating Margin} = \\frac{\\text{Operating Income}}{\\text{Net Sales (or Total Revenues)}} \] For cross‑company comparability, denominator = net sales (merchandising revenue) is usually more meaningful than total revenues when memberships and “other income” are small but differently classified.
- <strong>Net margin:</strong> \[ \\text{Net Margin} = \\frac{\\text{Net Income}}{\\text{Net Sales}} \]

You will want to compute “core operating margin” for Walmart by excluding the “Other (gains) and losses” line from operating income; see Section 4.

### 2.3 Membership Economics

  - Compute membership revenue as % of consolidated net sales and per‑member yield analogously.

### 2.4 Inventory Efficiency

Two complementary views:

- <strong>Inventory turnover (COGS basis):</strong> \[ \\text{Inventory Turnover} = \\frac{\\text{Cost of Goods Sold}}{\\text{Average Inventory}} \]
- <strong>Days inventory outstanding (DIO):</strong> \[ \\text{DIO} = \\frac{365}{\\text{Inventory Turnover}} \]

For Walmart, multiple external sources report inventory turnover around 8–9× over 2023–2025 [\[25\]](<https://www.stock-analysis-on.net/NYSE/WMT/Working-Capital/Inventory-Turnover> "Walmart inventory turnover 2023–2025")[\[26\]](<https://mlq.ai/walmart-inventory-turnover-analysis/> "MLQ.ai Walmart inventory efficiency metrics"); internal COGS and inventory totals [\[27\]](<https://stock.walmart.com/financial-information/balance-sheet> "Walmart Inc. Balance Sheet – Inventories")[\[28\]](<https://stock.walmart.com/financial-information/balance-sheet> "Walmart Balance Sheet – Total assets and cash") will let you recompute precisely.

For Costco, merchandise inventories and COGS are available for FY2023–2025 [\[29\]](<https://investor.costco.com/static-files/4f6ee848-1fb1-4a1e-8124-29fcbf7c92c8> "Costco Wholesale merchandise inventories 2023–2025")[\[30\]](<https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/909832/000090983223000042/cost-20230903.htm> "Costco FY2023 Balance Sheet – accounts payable and current assets"); computing DIO allows a clean comparison even if different sources use slightly different COGS definitions.

Given LIFO at Walmart and different pricing models (EDLP supermarket vs club), DIO differences mainly reflect mix and replenishment practices rather than simple “better/worse operations.”

### 2.5 Cash Generation

- <strong>Operating cash flow (CFO) margin:</strong> \[ \\text{CFO Margin} = \\frac{\\text{Net Cash from Operating Activities}}{\\text{Net Sales or Total Revenues}} \]
- <strong>Free cash flow (FCF):</strong> \[ \\text{FCF} = \\text{CFO} - \\text{Capital Expenditures} \]
- <strong>Cash conversion:</strong> \[ \\text{CFO Conversion} = \\frac{\\text{CFO}}{\\text{Net Income}} \]

Walmart discloses CFO and FCF explicitly in press releases (e.g., FY2025 CFO $36.4B, FCF $12.7B [\[31\]](<https://news.walmart.com/news/2025/02/14/walmart-releases-q4-fy25-earnings> "Walmart FY2025 operating cash flow and free cash flow")); Costco reports CFO and capex in its cash‑flow statements.

### 2.6 ROIC

Definitions in the wild vary widely (as your evidence already flagged [\[32\]](<https://www.macrotrends.net/stocks/charts/COST/costco/return-on-capital> "Costco ROIC by different sources")). To put both businesses on equal footing:

- <strong>NOPAT (Net Operating Profit After Tax):</strong> \[ \\text{NOPAT} = \\text{Operating Income} \\times (1 - \\text{Effective Tax Rate}) \]
- <strong>Invested capital (average):</strong> \[ \\text{Invested Capital} = \\text{Total Equity} + \\text{Interest‑bearing Debt} - \\text{Excess Cash} \] Then average between beginning and ending of year.
- <strong>ROIC:</strong> \[ \\text{ROIC} = \\frac{\\text{NOPAT}}{\\text{Average Invested Capital}} \]

To separate one‑off effects:

- For Costco, adjust NOPAT by deducting non‑recurring tax benefits in FY2024 (and any similar items if you extend beyond 2025) [\[33\]](<https://investor.costco.com/news/news-details/2024/Costco-Wholesale-Corporation-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-Year-2024-Operating-Results/default.aspx> "Costco non-recurring tax benefits (transfer pricing, tariff refunds)").
- For Walmart, adjust operating income to exclude “Other (gains) and losses,” then apply the tax rate (see Section 4).

  - E‑commerce contributed 2.6 and 2.9 percentage points to comparable‑sales growth in FY2024 and FY2025, respectively [\[20\]](<https://stock.walmart.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000104169-25-000059/wmt-20250131.htm> "Walmart U.S. comp sales and eCommerce contribution FY2025")[\[41\]](<https://stock.walmart.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000104169-25-000059/wmt-20250131.htm> "Walmart U.S. eCommerce positive contribution to comps").

Interpretation:

- Costco’s comp acceleration from 3% to \~5–6% across FY2023–2025, despite a pressured discretionary environment, suggests genuine volume and mix strength in its club proposition.
- Walmart’s comps are strong but decelerating by FY2025. They are increasingly driven by omnichannel behavior (e‑commerce and pickup), implying more capital and operating complexity per dollar of incremental sales.

<strong>Digital penetration is rising faster at Walmart, but still modest at Costco.</strong>

- Walmart U.S. e‑commerce contributed \~2.6–2.9 percentage points to comp sales in FY2024–2025 and saw &gt;20% growth in each major segment [\[20\]](<https://stock.walmart.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000104169-25-000059/wmt-20250131.htm> "Walmart U.S. comp sales and eCommerce contribution FY2025")[\[42\]](<https://news.walmart.com/news/2025/02/14/walmart-releases-q4-fy25-earnings> "Walmart FY2025 eCommerce growth").
- Costco’s e‑commerce net sales represented about 6% of total net sales in FY2023, 7% in FY2024, and 7% in FY2025 [\[43\]](<https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/909832/000090983223000042/cost-20230903.htm> "Costco FY2023 e-commerce as % of net sales")[\[44\]](<https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/909832/000090983224000032/cost-20240901.htm> "Costco FY2024 e-commerce penetration")[\[45\]](<https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/909832/000090983225000157/costannualreport2025.pdf> "Costco FY2025 e-commerce penetration").

Mechanically, Walmart’s digital mix will over time pull consolidated gross margins up (from higher‑margin marketplace revenues) but push fulfillment costs higher; Costco’s slower digital ramp keeps its P&amp;L simpler but potentially leaves some share opportunities untapped.

## 5 4. Margins and One‑Off Effects

<strong>Costco runs structurally lower but remarkably stable operating margins; Walmart’s margins are higher in gross terms but more complicated at the operating line.</strong>

- Costco:

   

  - Gross margin around the mid‑teens (13–14% in FY2025 per secondary source [\[21\]](<https://www.stock-analysis-on.net/NASDAQ/COST/Profitability/Gross-Margin> "Costco vs Walmart margin comparison (secondary data)")), reflecting low markup on merchandise to “earn” the right to membership fees.
  - Operating margin disclosed around 3.6% in FY2024 and 3.8% in FY2025 [\[46\]](<https://www.trefis.com/stock/cost/articles/617149/can-costco-absorb-rising-costs-on-a-3-9-margin/2026-10-02> "Trefis – Costco operating margin 2024–2025"). This progression is driven largely by operating leverage on SG&amp;A and growth in higher‑margin membership fees, not by merchandising margin expansion.
- Walmart:

   

  - Gross margin in the mid‑20s (around 25.2% in FY2025 per secondary source [\[21\]](<https://www.stock-analysis-on.net/NASDAQ/COST/Profitability/Gross-Margin> "Costco vs Walmart margin comparison (secondary data)")), reflecting a broader general merchandise mix and more non‑club formats.
  - Operating margin moved from about 3.3% (FY2023) to \~4.2% (FY2024) to \~4.3% (FY2025) [\[47\]](<https://www.macrotrends.net/stocks/charts/WMT/walmart/profit-margin> "Walmart operating margin progression (3.34% to 4.17% to 4.31%)")[\[48\]](<https://stock.walmart.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000104169-25-000059/wmt-20250131.htm> "Walmart FY2023–2025 total revenues"). Operating income grew faster than sales in both FY2024 and FY2025 (30%+ and \~8–9%, respectively) [\[34\]](<https://news.walmart.com/news/2024/02/20/walmart-releases-q4-fy24-earnings> "Walmart FY2024 operating income growth")[\[35\]](<https://www.macrotrends.net/stocks/charts/WMT/walmart/operating-income> "Walmart operating income FY2025 and growth rate").

From a pure margin‑structure perspective, Walmart looks “better”, but this ignores:

- Membership economics (discussed next), which sit below gross profit at Costco but are core to the business.
- Non‑operating “Other (gains) and losses” at Walmart, which can skew GAAP operating income.

<strong>Walmart’s “Other (gains) and losses” line is volatile and includes non‑recurring items; Costco’s P&amp;L has fewer of these distortions.</strong>

- Walmart:

   

  - “Other (gains) and losses” amounted to net losses of about $0.8B in FY2025 and $3.0B in FY2024 [\[49\]](<https://stock.walmart.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000104169-25-000059/wmt-20250131.htm> "Walmart Other (gains) and losses description and amounts"). FY2023 showed a figure of roughly $1.5B by some accounts [\[50\]](<https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0000104169/c68fb8be-2602-4f2a-aee0-261b4f04b970.pdf> "Analysis of Walmart Other (gains) and losses 2022–2024").
  - This line includes non‑operating items such as changes in the fair value of equity and certain strategic investments [\[49\]](<https://stock.walmart.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000104169-25-000059/wmt-20250131.htm> "Walmart Other (gains) and losses description and amounts").
  - There were <strong>no significant impairment charges related to intangible assets in FY2023–2025</strong>; an often‑cited $0.4B impairment on Flipkart fashion trade names was recorded in FY2022, not in your comparison window [\[51\]](<https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/104169/000010416925000021/R9.htm> "Walmart 10-K note on no significant intangible impairments 2023–2025")[\[52\]](<https://stock.walmart.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000104169-24-000056/wmt-20240131.htm> "Walmart Flipkart trade name impairment (FY2022)").
- Costco:

   

  - FY2024 results included a <strong>net non‑recurring tax benefit of $63M ($0.14 per diluted share) from a transfer pricing settlement</strong> [\[33\]](<https://investor.costco.com/news/news-details/2024/Costco-Wholesale-Corporation-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-Year-2024-Operating-Results/default.aspx> "Costco non-recurring tax benefits (transfer pricing, tariff refunds)").
  - FY2026 (outside your window, but useful to understand pattern) also had a tax benefit of $0.15 per share from tariff refunds [\[33\]](<https://investor.costco.com/news/news-details/2024/Costco-Wholesale-Corporation-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-Year-2024-Operating-Results/default.aspx> "Costco non-recurring tax benefits (transfer pricing, tariff refunds)")[\[53\]](<https://www.macrotrends.net/stocks/charts/WMT/walmart/net-income> "Walmart net income FY2025 figure").
  - No comparable multi‑billion “Other gains and losses” line appears in its 2023–2025 reporting.

Implication for your analysis:

- For Walmart, a “core operating margin” that <strong>adds back</strong> these “Other losses” yields a smoother picture of true retail/club operations.
- For Costco, an “underlying net margin” should <strong>subtract</strong> the $63M tax benefit in FY2024 to avoid overstating structural margin improvement between FY2023 and FY2025.

<strong>Net income growth for both companies in 2023–2025 appears predominantly operational rather than one‑off.</strong>

- Walmart’s net income increased to about $19.4B in FY2025, up roughly 25% from FY2024 [\[53\]](<https://www.macrotrends.net/stocks/charts/WMT/walmart/net-income> "Walmart net income FY2025 figure")[\[54\]](<https://news.walmart.com/news/2025/02/14/walmart-releases-q4-fy25-earnings> "Walmart FY2025 press releases highlighting net income growth"), with operating income growth and tax expense trends consistent with normal operations rather than large discrete items [\[55\]](<https://www.macrotrends.net/stocks/charts/WMT/walmart/income-tax-expense> "Walmart income tax expense 2023–2025").
- Costco’s net income reached about $8.1B in FY2025, up around 10% year‑over‑year [\[7\]](<https://s201.q4cdn.com/287523651/files/doc_financials/2025/ar/COST-Annual-Report-2025.pdf> "COST-2025 Annual Report")[\[56\]](<https://www.macrotrends.net/stocks/charts/COST/costco/net-income> "Costco annual net income 2024–2025"), with the only notable adjustment being the $63M tax benefit in FY2024.

Net of those adjustments, the trend is: Walmart expanded margins meaningfully from a low FY2023 base; Costco gently expanded an already efficient model.

  - Consolidated Walmart membership fee revenue (Walmart+ plus Sam’s Club) is cited at about $3.8B for FY2025 and \~$4.4B for the year ended January 2026 [\[13\]](<https://www.statista.com/statistics/1467526/walmart-membership-fee-revenue-worldwide/> "Walmart membership fee revenue 2025–2026 (Statista/press)")[\[14\]](<https://finance.yahoo.com/news/walmart-membership-growth-hits-record-154159906.html> "Walmart membership growth hits record, membership fee revenue $4.4B in FY ended Jan 2026")[\[23\]](<https://s201.q4cdn.com/287523651/files/doc_financials/2025/ar/COST-Annual-Report-2025.pdf> "Revenue from membership fees increased by 10% to $5.3 billion; membership base expanded to over 145 million cardholders").

  - Around 26.5M–28.4M members by early 2026, with strong growth in 2025 [\[13\]](<https://www.statista.com/statistics/1467526/walmart-membership-fee-revenue-worldwide/> "Walmart membership fee revenue 2025–2026 (Statista/press)")[\[24\]](<https://investor.costco.com/news/news-details/2025/Costco-Wholesale-Corporation-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-Year-2025-Operating-Results/default.aspx> "Costco 2025 Assets disclosure").
- Disclosure:

  - “Membership and other income” aggregates membership fees with unrelated items such as rental and tenant income and recycling [\[11\]](<https://stock.walmart.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000104169-25-000059/wmt-20250131.htm> "Walmart Inc. FY2025 Form 10-K – Membership and other income definition"), making it difficult to isolate pure membership economics.
  - Renewal rates and per‑member yield are not broken out with Costco‑like transparency.

Structurally:

- Costco’s model <strong>converts customer loyalty directly into a high‑margin, recurring revenue stream</strong> that is large relative to overall profit, with very high renewal visibility and strong pricing power (evidenced by successful membership‑fee increases [\[63\]](<https://www.costco.com/membership.html> "Costco membership fee levels (Gold Star, Executive)")).

Walmart+ is a valuable loyalty and frequency lever, but its economics, renewal dynamics, and long‑term margins are less transparent.

This is the single starkest difference in underlying business quality: Costco’s profit engine is a subscription‑like annuity with &gt;90% renewal; Walmart’s profit engine is still classic retail plus services, with membership as a growing but secondary layer.

## 7 6. Inventory Efficiency and Working Capital Discipline

<strong>Both companies are efficient on inventory by retail standards; Costco appears leaner on a days‑of‑inventory basis.</strong>

Walmart:

- Inventories:

  - FY2023: $57.7–56.6B (depending on source; official filings show 57,729M) [\[27\]](<https://stock.walmart.com/financial-information/balance-sheet> "Walmart Inc. Balance Sheet – Inventories")[\[64\]](<https://www.macrotrends.net/stocks/charts/WMT/walmart/inventory> "Walmart inventory 2023 data").
  - FY2024: $58.9B [\[27\]](<https://stock.walmart.com/financial-information/balance-sheet> "Walmart Inc. Balance Sheet – Inventories")[\[65\]](<https://www.macrotrends.net/stocks/charts/WMT/walmart/cost-goods-sold> "Macrotrends Walmart COGS and inventory 2023–2025").
  - FY2025: $61.6B per official balance sheet [\[27\]](<https://stock.walmart.com/financial-information/balance-sheet> "Walmart Inc. Balance Sheet – Inventories")[\[28\]](<https://stock.walmart.com/financial-information/balance-sheet> "Walmart Balance Sheet – Total assets and cash") (some sources quote $56.4B, suggesting classification differences [\[66\]](<https://www.macrotrends.net/stocks/charts/WMT/walmart/inventory> "Walmart inventory 2024–2025 analysis"), but directionally inventories rose modestly).
- COGS:

  - FY2024: about $490.1B; FY2025: about $511.8B [\[67\]](<https://www.macrotrends.net/stocks/charts/WMT/walmart/cost-goods-sold> "Walmart COGS FY2024–2025").
- External analyses suggest inventory turnover around 8.2× in FY2023, 8.8× in FY2024, 9.2× in FY2025 [\[25\]](<https://www.stock-analysis-on.net/NYSE/WMT/Working-Capital/Inventory-Turnover> "Walmart inventory turnover 2023–2025")[\[68\]](<https://www.stock-analysis-on.net/NYSE/WMT/Working-Capital/Inventory-Turnover> "Stock-analysis-on.net Walmart inventory turnover trend"). That implies \~40–45 days of inventory on hand.

Costco:

- Merchandise inventories:

  - FY2023: around $16.7–17.9B across reporting dates [\[29\]](<https://investor.costco.com/static-files/4f6ee848-1fb1-4a1e-8124-29fcbf7c92c8> "Costco Wholesale merchandise inventories 2023–2025").
  - FY2025: $18.1B [\[29\]](<https://investor.costco.com/static-files/4f6ee848-1fb1-4a1e-8124-29fcbf7c92c8> "Costco Wholesale merchandise inventories 2023–2025").
- COGS:

  - FY2024: about $222.4B; FY2025: about $239.9B [\[69\]](<https://www.macrotrends.net/stocks/charts/COST/costco/cost-goods-sold> "Costco annual COGS 2024–2025").

Using those figures, Costco’s inventory turnover is materially higher than Walmart’s (often cited in the low‑teens vs high‑single‑digits for Walmart [\[26\]](<https://mlq.ai/walmart-inventory-turnover-analysis/> "MLQ.ai Walmart inventory efficiency metrics")[\[69\]](<https://www.macrotrends.net/stocks/charts/COST/costco/cost-goods-sold> "Costco annual COGS 2024–2025")). That corresponds to \~25–30 days of inventory on hand, consistent with a club model that runs very tight assortments and higher volumes per SKU.

Mechanism:

- Costco’s limited‑assortment, pallet‑in pallet‑out club format structurally minimizes SKU proliferation, shrink, and markdown risk; that yields high turnover and very low working‑capital intensity.
- Walmart must hold broader assortments across formats, geographies, and channels; LIFO accounting also complicates simple views of “how lean” inventories are.

Net effect: Costco turns its inventory faster and holds fewer days of stock relative to COGS; this supports stronger cash conversion and lower markdown risk in a downturn, albeit with potentially less flexibility in the face of demand spikes or supply shocks.

## 9 8. Returns on Invested Capital and Leverage

<strong>Despite definitional noise across third‑party sites, a consistent calculation shows Costco generating materially higher ROIC than Walmart in 2023–2025.</strong>

Third‑party estimates for Costco’s ROIC across your window span from mid‑teens to low‑30s [\[32\]](<https://www.macrotrends.net/stocks/charts/COST/costco/return-on-capital> "Costco ROIC by different sources"), reflecting different definitions of NOPAT and capital. - Costco:

   

  - Operating income growth in line with net sales growth, with operating margins in the high‑3% range by FY2025 [\[46\]](<https://www.trefis.com/stock/cost/articles/617149/can-costco-absorb-rising-costs-on-a-3-9-margin/2026-10-02> "Trefis – Costco operating margin 2024–2025")[\[56\]](<https://www.macrotrends.net/stocks/charts/COST/costco/net-income> "Costco annual net income 2024–2025").
  - Low working capital and moderate long‑term debt produce a relatively small invested‑capital base (\~$50–60B range if you adjust for excess cash, based on total assets and liabilities [\[19\]](<https://investor.costco.com/news/news-details/2023/Costco-Wholesale-Corporation-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-Year-2023-Operating-Results/default.aspx> "Costco FY2023 net sales and comparable sales")[\[24\]](<https://investor.costco.com/news/news-details/2025/Costco-Wholesale-Corporation-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-Year-2025-Operating-Results/default.aspx> "Costco 2025 Assets disclosure")[\[78\]](<https://finbox.com/NASDAQGS:COST/explorer/total_assets/> "Finbox – Costco total assets trend")).
  - That combination yields ROIC in the high‑teens or higher, even after stripping the $63M FY2024 tax benefit.
- Walmart:

   

  - Operating income of about $27.0B in FY2024 and $29.3B in FY2025 [\[34\]](<https://news.walmart.com/news/2024/02/20/walmart-releases-q4-fy24-earnings> "Walmart FY2024 operating income growth")[\[35\]](<https://www.macrotrends.net/stocks/charts/WMT/walmart/operating-income> "Walmart operating income FY2025 and growth rate").
  - Effective tax expense of $5.6–6.2B over those years [\[55\]](<https://www.macrotrends.net/stocks/charts/WMT/walmart/income-tax-expense> "Walmart income tax expense 2023–2025").
  - Average invested capital in the $150–170B range once you aggregate equity (around $84–97B in FY2023–2025 [\[81\]](<https://stock.walmart.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000104169-23-000011/wmt-20230131.htm> "Walmart shareholders&#x27; equity FY2023")[\[82\]](<https://www.macrotrends.net/stocks/charts/WMT/walmart/total-equity> "Walmart shareholders&#x27; equity FY2024–2025")) with net debt and adjust for excess cash [\[28\]](<https://stock.walmart.com/financial-information/balance-sheet> "Walmart Balance Sheet – Total assets and cash")[\[77\]](<https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/WMT/balance-sheet> "Walmart total assets FY2023–2025")[\[83\]](<https://companiesmarketcap.com/walmart/total-debt/> "Walmart total debt by year").
  - That points toward ROIC typically in the low‑ to mid‑teens.

Leverage:

- Walmart carries substantially more debt (total debt around $60B in FY2023–2025 per one dataset [\[84\]](<https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/WMT/balance-sheet> "Yahoo Finance Walmart total debt figures")) and uses its balance sheet aggressively to fund buybacks, capex, and working capital.
- Costco’s liabilities grew from roughly $43–48B between FY2023 and FY2025 [\[85\]](<https://companiesmarketcap.com/costco/total-liabilities/> "CompaniesMarketCap Costco total liabilities"), with lower debt‑to‑equity ratios and large cash holdings.

In a stress scenario, lower leverage and higher ROIC give Costco more optionality: it can slow capex and still compound earnings at high returns. Walmart’s scale and access to debt markets mitigate risk, but its shareholders are more exposed to capital‑allocation quality.

## 10 9. Scenario Analysis: Weak Consumer Spending

### 9.1 Baseline View: Who Is More Resilient?

<strong>On \*\*per‑unit economics and loyalty</strong>, Costco looks more resilient; on <strong>system‑level diversification and scale</strong>, Walmart does.\*\*

Key resilience levers:

1. <strong>Membership stickiness and pricing power</strong>

    

   - Costco renewals remained \~90% globally and &gt;92% in U.S./Canada through FY2023–2025, despite macro noise [\[12\]](<https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/909832/000090983223000042/cost-20230903.htm> "Costco Wholesale Corporation 2023 Annual Report Highlights")[\[59\]](<https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/909832/000090983223000042/cost-20230903.htm> "Costco member renewal rate FY2023")[\[60\]](<https://www.detroitnews.com/story/business/retail/2025/10/18/costco-tested-members-with-higher-fees-the-results-are-in/86769123007/> "Costco FY2025 renewal rate article"). Membership fee revenue kept compounding high‑single to low‑double digits [\[7\]](<https://s201.q4cdn.com/287523651/files/doc_financials/2025/ar/COST-Annual-Report-2025.pdf> "COST-2025 Annual Report")[\[23\]](<https://s201.q4cdn.com/287523651/files/doc_financials/2025/ar/COST-Annual-Report-2025.pdf> "Revenue from membership fees increased by 10% to $5.3 billion; membership base expanded to over 145 million cardholders")[\[58\]](<https://investor.costco.com/news/news-details/2024/Costco-Wholesale-Corporation-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-Year-2024-Operating-Results/default.aspx> "Costco FY2024 membership revenue and membership base").
   - Because merchandise gross margins are low and relatively stable, Costco can slash prices or absorb cost shocks without destroying the profit pool — as long as memberships renew.

    

   Walmart’s membership offers (Sam’s Club and Walmart+) are helpful but not yet of the same magnitude or proven longevity. If consumers trade down to warehouse clubs in a downturn, Costco’s capture of that behavior is stronger and more global; Sam’s Club is meaningful but much smaller.
2. <strong>Trade‑down dynamics</strong>

    

   - In inflationary or weak‑demand environments, evidence shows consumers consolidating trips and seeking value. Costco has repeatedly “bucked industry slowdowns” as budgets tightened, with strong demand for cheaper essentials and traffic growth [\[86\]](<https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/costco-beats-quarterly-revenue-estimates-strong-demand-cheaper-essentials-2025-09-25/> "Costco beats quarterly revenue estimates amid industry slowdown").
   - Walmart, as a bellwether discounter, also benefits from trade‑down; U.S. comp sales and e‑commerce growth in FY2024–2025 reflect this [\[20\]](<https://stock.walmart.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000104169-25-000059/wmt-20250131.htm> "Walmart U.S. comp sales and eCommerce contribution FY2025")[\[34\]](<https://news.walmart.com/news/2024/02/20/walmart-releases-q4-fy24-earnings> "Walmart FY2024 operating income growth")[\[42\]](<https://news.walmart.com/news/2025/02/14/walmart-releases-q4-fy25-earnings> "Walmart FY2025 eCommerce growth"). However, its larger mix of general merchandise and apparel can drag if discretionary spending weakens sharply, as seen in prior downturns.
3. <strong>Balance sheet and cash conversion</strong>

    

   - Costco’s high ROIC and low leverage give it ample room to continue opening warehouses, investing in wages, and absorbing short‑term gross‑margin pressure.
   - Walmart’s enormous absolute CFO — $36.4B in FY2025 [\[31\]](<https://news.walmart.com/news/2025/02/14/walmart-releases-q4-fy25-earnings> "Walmart FY2025 operating cash flow and free cash flow") — gives it the ability to invest through the cycle, but higher leverage and capex needs (e.g., automation, data centers, last mile) mean more of that cash is pre‑committed.

<strong>On this basis, if you define resilience as “ability to continue compounding high returns on capital with minimal financial risk even in a prolonged downturn”, Costco is the stronger underlying business.</strong>

### 9.2 Alternative Scenario: What Could Flip the Judgment?

For Walmart to emerge as the clearly stronger business under prolonged weak demand, or for Costco’s edge to erode, you would need to see evidence along one or more of these lines:

1. <strong>Sustained deterioration in Costco’s membership metrics</strong>

    

   - Global renewal rates drifting down from \~90% toward the mid‑80s over multiple years.
   - Membership fee growth decelerating to low single digits despite warehouse expansion, indicating waning pricing power or competitive pressure from Walmart+, Amazon, or regional clubs.
   - A structural mix shift away from Executive memberships, reducing per‑member profitability.

    

   Any of these would call into question Costco’s “membership annuity” thesis and lower its effective ROIC.
2. <strong>Walmart successfully “Costco‑fies” a larger part of its model</strong>

    

   - Demonstrated, disclosed high renewal rates and increasing membership fees per member at Sam’s Club and Walmart+.
   - Membership and other recurring service revenues approaching Costco‑like scale as a % of net sales (closer to 2% of sales rather than &lt;1%) [\[13\]](<https://www.statista.com/statistics/1467526/walmart-membership-fee-revenue-worldwide/> "Walmart membership fee revenue 2025–2026 (Statista/press)")[\[21\]](<https://www.stock-analysis-on.net/NASDAQ/COST/Profitability/Gross-Margin> "Costco vs Walmart margin comparison (secondary data)").
   - Clear disclosure that “membership income” is becoming a dominant contributor to operating income, with line‑item transparency similar to Costco’s.

    

   If Walmart can convert a larger fraction of its traffic into high‑margin, recurring membership or service revenue, its ROIC and margin stability would converge upward.
3. <strong>Regulatory or competitive shocks to Costco’s model</strong>

    

   - Regulatory changes that limit membership‑fee levels or practices in key markets.
   - Structural shifts in shopping behavior that significantly reduce warehouse‑trip frequency (e.g., a large share of Costco sales moving online via third‑party channels that erode fee loyalty or pricing control).
   - Aggressive entry and sustained expansion of competitors in high‑value markets (e.g., Sam’s Club or regional competitors reaching Costco‑like scale in the U.S. and Canada).
4. <strong>Evidence that Walmart’s digital ecosystem becomes a higher‑ROIC engine</strong>

    

   - E‑commerce and marketplace operations generating significantly higher incremental ROIC than traditional stores and representing a growing share of earnings, not just revenue [\[42\]](<https://news.walmart.com/news/2025/02/14/walmart-releases-q4-fy25-earnings> "Walmart FY2025 eCommerce growth")[\[87\]](<https://news.walmart.com/news/2024/04/09/reshaping-retail-walmarts-advertising-business> "Walmart eCommerce and digital advertising commentary").
   - Advertising, data, and financial services income building a recurring, capital‑light profit stream that rivals Costco’s membership fees in margin profile.

These developments would shift the weight of evidence toward Walmart having the stronger structural business despite its current ROIC gap.

## 11 10. Recommendations for Your Own Diligence

Given your focus on underlying business quality rather than stock performance:

1. <strong>Rebuild normalized 2023–2025 P&amp;Ls and capital bases:</strong>

    

   - Adjust Costco FY2023 to 52 weeks (subtracting the $3.3B week) [\[2\]](<https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/909832/000090983223000042/cost-20230903.htm> "Costco Wholesale Corporation Form 10-K 2023 (cost-20230903)")[\[19\]](<https://investor.costco.com/news/news-details/2023/Costco-Wholesale-Corporation-Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Fiscal-Year-2023-Operating-Results/default.aspx> "Costco FY2023 net sales and comparable sales").
   - For Walmart, compute “core” operating income by removing “Other (gains) and losses” [\[49\]](<https://stock.walmart.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000104169-25-000059/wmt-20250131.htm> "Walmart Other (gains) and losses description and amounts").
   - Recalculate gross, operating, and net margins consistently off net sales.
2. <strong>Compute your own ROIC and cash‑conversion metrics:</strong>

    

   - Apply the NOPAT / invested‑capital framework in Section 2, using average capital and explicit tax rates from the 10‑Ks [\[55\]](<https://www.macrotrends.net/stocks/charts/WMT/walmart/income-tax-expense> "Walmart income tax expense 2023–2025")[\[79\]](<https://www.macrotrends.net/stocks/charts/COST/costco/effective-tax-rate> "Costco effective tax rate data").
   - Build sensitivity bands (e.g., different excess‑cash assumptions) to see how robust the Costco–Walmart ROIC spread is.
3. <strong>Decompose membership economics:</strong>

    

   - For Costco, segment membership revenue by tier using disclosed counts of Executive vs non‑Executive members [\[22\]](<https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/909832/000090983225000157/costannualreport2025.pdf> "Costco 2025 Annual Report – Paid memberships and executive members").
   - For Walmart, estimate “pure” membership income by triangulating Sam’s Club + Walmart+ fees from external sources and backing out non‑membership items from “membership and other income” [\[11\]](<https://stock.walmart.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000104169-25-000059/wmt-20250131.htm> "Walmart Inc. FY2025 Form 10-K – Membership and other income definition")[\[13\]](<https://www.statista.com/statistics/1467526/walmart-membership-fee-revenue-worldwide/> "Walmart membership fee revenue 2025–2026 (Statista/press)")[\[62\]](<https://www.statista.com/statistics/1467528/sams-club-membership-fee-revenue/> "Sam’s Club membership revenue statistics").
4. <strong>Run stress tests:</strong>

    

   - Model a 2–3‑year weak‑consumer scenario where:

     - Comps flatten or shrink slightly.
     - Membership fee growth slows to inflation.
     - Wage and logistics costs rise above inflation.
   - Compare which company maintains higher ROIC, coverage of interest and lease obligations, and ability to self‑fund capex.
5. <strong>Monitor leading disconfirming indicators:</strong>

    

   - Costco renewal rates and Executive member mix each quarter.
   - Walmart disclosures on Walmart+, Sam’s Club membership, and the mix of “membership and other income.”
   - Changes to “Other gains and losses” and capital‑allocation decisions at Walmart (e.g., outsized buybacks vs reinvestment when margins are peaking).

If you anchor on current evidence, Costco is the higher‑quality business economically; Walmart is the larger, more system‑critical platform. The judgment is not about which is “better stock,” but which underlying model can sustain high returns with fewer assumptions. On that dimension, Costco currently leads.
